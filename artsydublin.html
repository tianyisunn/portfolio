01
The Gap

Problem framing

The journey did not end when the ticket was booked.

Artsy started with a simple problem: cultural platforms are good at getting people to an event, but weak at what happens next. After checkout, attendance becomes a memory the platform cannot see.

There is no easy place to keep a record, build a cultural identity or find someone with similar interests. That is the participation continuity gap.

Participation continuity gapArtsy is not only a way to buy a ticket. It is a layer that helps people discover, remember and continue participating in Dublin's cultural life.
02
The Journey

Research synthesis

The low point came after the event, not before it.

Mapping the full experience changed the question. Discovery and attendance were already visible. The missing part was the quiet work afterwards: recording what happened, building a sense of taste and finding people to share the next event with.

01DiscoverFind an event that fits my interests, budget and schedule.
02BookUnderstand the event and complete the ticket handoff.
03AttendEnjoy the experience and feel part of the local scene.
04LogKeep a quick record instead of letting the memory disappear.
05BuildLet past events shape a visible cultural profile.
06ConnectFind shared interests and make a low-pressure connection.
03
The Structure

Information architecture

We connected discovery, memory and connection.

The sitemap turned the gap into a product structure. Home helps people discover. Profile keeps participation visible. Inbox supports the reminders and conversations that make the next visit easier.

HomeKeep discovery easy to scan

All Events, For You and user-created events sit together because people need both reliable listings and a sense of what the community is doing.

ProfileMake participation accumulate

History, saved events, posts and created events turn a profile into a record of participation rather than a static identity card.

InboxKeep the next step visible

Reminders, ticket updates and messages give the experience a place to continue after the user leaves an event page.

04
The Decisions

From sketch to system

The early sketches changed the product's centre.

The first sketches were useful because they showed where the experience was still fragmented. We used them to decide which screens should carry the continuity of the product.

01
Homepage

Keep the feed-led structure, but make For You depend on interests and participation rather than a generic list.

02
User page

Move beyond an avatar and bio. Past events, posts and saved plans should show how a person takes part.

03
Event page

Support the decision to go, the ticket handoff and the possibility of meeting someone around the same event.

Every feature had to answer one question: what helps the user continue after the event?

05
The Visual System

Visual design

A neutral interface lets the events keep their colour.

Event posters already bring colour, imagery and strong visual personalities. If the interface used another loud colour system, it would compete with the content. We kept the product neutral so the event itself could lead.

Neutral base

Cream, brown and muted green give the interface a stable background for very different event posters.

Readable details

Body text and event metadata stay quiet and scannable, especially when the content around them is visually dense.

Controlled emphasis

Display type adds character to the brand without turning every screen into a competing visual statement.

06
The Build

From product logic to interface

The system had to support the whole loop.

Because the product connects events, people and histories, the build could not stop at a set of static screens. The front-end needed to make those relationships visible and keep the experience usable across the main journeys.

01Discover

Event cards, categories and For You content make the first decision easier to scan.

02Record

History and saved events give participation a place to accumulate.

03Connect

Posts, messages and event-specific relationships support a lighter form of social use.

React

Built reusable event, profile and feed components so the same information could appear across different journeys.

MySQL

Structured the relationships between users, events, saved items, attendance history and posts.

GitHub

Used for version control, team collaboration and keeping design and implementation changes traceable.

08
What Comes Next

Outcome and reflection

What we made, and what still needs testing.

The prototype moved Artsy beyond event discovery. It connected the moments that usually sit apart: choosing something to attend, remembering it afterwards and finding a reason to return.

EstablishedA clearer way to connect event discovery with personal history and future recommendations.
Still openWhether people will return after an event to record it, and what would make that habit feel worthwhile.
Next testMeasure repeat participation, diary use and whether event-based connections become meaningful over time.

The next question is not whether people can find an event. It is whether Artsy gives them a reason to come back.

Artsy is built around what happens next.

  • From discovery to attendance, the product keeps the journey visible
  • Past events become part of a cultural profile rather than disappearing
  • Social connection happens around a shared event, not a generic group
  • The next iteration should test whether people return after attending
