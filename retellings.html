01. The Problem

James Joyce is a cornerstone of Irish heritage, yet a significant engagement gap exists in physical exhibitions. Traditional museum displays often rely on static text and artifacts, which can feel inaccessible to younger, non-expert audiences. The challenge was: How might we transform the action viewing into active literary interaction for the modern traveler, book worm, and history lover?

02. The Insight: "The Context Switching Barrier"

Through field investigations at the Dublin Writers Museum and user interviews, we discovered that 80% of visitors felt "cognitive overload." The barrier wasn't the complexity of Joyce's prose, but the constant need to switch attention between a physical plaque and their imagination. 03. The Idea: A Living Narrative Overlay

We proposed "Retellings"—a mobile-first web experience that uses minimalist visual metaphors and location-aware triggers. By simplifying complex stream-of-consciousness into "glanceable" UI, we aimed to lower the barrier to entry for Joyce’s Dubliners and turn the city into a gallery.

04. Testing: Balancing Safety & Immersion

During our initial testing phase with low-fidelity wireframes, we found that heavy Augmented Reality (AR) visual overlays distracted users from the physical beauty of Dublin. Users reported safety concerns while navigating busy streets. This feedback was a crucial turning point for our design direction.

05. Iterate: Shifting to Audio-First

We iterated by shifting to an "Audio-First, Visual-Second" approach. I refactored the front-end to prioritize clean, accessible interfaces that complement the surroundings rather than competing with them. The result was a 30% increase in user dwell time during our pilot walkthrough, proving that "less is more" in environmental UX.

Technical Implementation

As the primary front-end developer, I ensured all code comments and documentation were written in English to maintain professional standards. I focused on Responsive Design and Clean Code principles, ensuring the web interface remained performant across various mobile devices during field use.
